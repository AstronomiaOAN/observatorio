469|A tower between botany and the sky
470|The Observatory's story begins in a world where measuring the sky was also a way of understanding the land.
471|Its origins are linked to José Celestino Mutis and the Royal Botanical Expedition. Historical studies document astronomical and meteorological activity before the building existed: Mutis's records, Francisco José de Caldas's work and their exchanges with Alexander von Humboldt help reconstruct that context.
472|Alberto Gómez Gutiérrez's work examines this background through documents, diaries and correspondence. The tower emerged from a network of scientific practices connecting observation, travel, geography and collections.
473|Alberto Gómez Gutiérrez: the OAN's background ↗
474|Built by Friar Domingo de Petrés, the historic observatory has a distinctive octagonal plan. The Señal Memoria archive preserves Benjamín Calvo's explanation of its architecture and images of the building in central Bogotá.
475|Señal Memoria: history and audiovisual archive ↗
476|A history in motion
477|The Observatory is built
478|Construction, promoted by Mutis, begins in May 1802 and ends on August 20, 1803, within the setting of the Botanical Expedition.
479|Reference document ↗
480|Observing to measure time
481|A documentary study published in 2025 analyzes solar observations, magnetic declination measurements and observations of Jupiter's satellites from April to September 1804.
482|The Caldas period
483|Caldas settles in Santafé in late 1805 and carries out sustained astronomical and meteorological work from 1806.
484|19th century
485|Science and republican history
486|The building experiences interruptions and changes of use. The careers of Liévano, González Benito and Garavito reflect different efforts to strengthen astronomical practice.
487|The university connection
488|Sources distinguish incorporation into Universidad Nacional in 1867 from its ratification in 1936; this distinction matters in the institution's history.
489|A location on the university campus
490|The campus observatory is built, expanding facilities for academic and observational work.
491|Heritage recognition
492|The historic observatory is declared a National Monument. Its conservation connects architecture with the memory of science.
493|Graduate education
494|The 2023 institutional editorial dates the start of graduate teaching and research activity to 1999.
495|eSPECTRA is launched
496|The inaugural issue accompanies the 220th anniversary and opens a publishing space for the community.
497|The garden is restored
498|A project with Bogotá's Botanical Garden renews the vegetation and its interpretation as heritage.
499|Instruments: other ways to read the past
500|An instrument preserves more than an observing technique. It invites questions about what was measured, how people learned to use it, who built it and what results it produced. Studying the relationship between objects and documents is essential to understanding astronomical heritage.
501|Clocks and chronometers
502|The analysis of the 1804 observations identifies solar records presumably intended to determine how fast or slow a chronometer and pendulum clock were running. Timekeeping was thus closely connected to observing the sky.
503|Read the documentary study ↗
504|Telescopes and measurements
505|The same study analyzes observations of Jupiter's satellites intended to refine geographical longitude, as well as magnetic declination measurements. Documents help reconstruct practices even when the identity of the observers remains debated.
506|Read the research ↗
507|Research into telescopes, clocks, compasses, sextants and other historical instruments should compare inventories, inscriptions, photographs and records of use. These examples guide research; they are not an inventory of objects currently on display.
508|This website does not replace a museum catalogue. An object's identification, maker, date, provenance and condition must be supported by its documentary record.
509|A heritage that is also alive
510|The garden restoration, planned in 2024 and carried out in 2025, resulted from a partnership between the OAN and Bogotá's Botanical Garden. The project introduced over 1,400 plants, including six new trees, and renewed the site's symbolic dimension.
511|The design connects species associated with botanical tradition with the memory of Mutis, Humboldt and Garavito. It links astronomy, cultural landscape and environmental education.

539|How to research this heritage
540|Locate
541|Start with the catalogue and record the title, date, author, collection and reference number. A bibliographic record does not mean that the document has been digitized.
542|Compare
543|Distinguish the document's date from the event's date. Compare manuscripts, publications and studies before attributing an observation or object.
544|Cite and preserve
545|Ask the custodian about consultation and reproduction conditions. Preserve the provenance of images and transcriptions so others can verify them.
546|The OAN Heritage Group works on tangible and intangible heritage. For enquiries about collections and projects, use the contact details in its profile.
