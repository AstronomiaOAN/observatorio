A tower between botany and the sky

The Observatory's story begins in a world where measuring the sky was also a way of understanding the land.

Its origins are linked to José Celestino Mutis and the Royal Botanical Expedition. Historical studies document astronomical and meteorological activity before the building existed: Mutis's records, Francisco José de Caldas's work and their exchanges with Alexander von Humboldt help reconstruct that context.

Alberto Gómez Gutiérrez's work examines this background through documents, diaries and correspondence. The tower emerged from a network of scientific practices connecting observation, travel, geography and collections.

Source: Alberto Gómez Gutiérrez: the OAN's background ↗.

Built by Friar Domingo de Petrés, the historic observatory has a distinctive octagonal plan. The Señal Memoria archive preserves Benjamín Calvo's explanation of its architecture and images of the building in central Bogotá.

Source: Señal Memoria: history and audiovisual archive ↗.

Instruments: other ways to read the past

An instrument preserves more than an observing technique. It invites questions about what was measured, how people learned to use it, who built it and what results it produced. Studying the relationship between objects and documents is essential to understanding astronomical heritage.

Clocks and chronometers

The analysis of the 1804 observations identifies solar records presumably intended to determine how fast or slow a chronometer and pendulum clock were running. Timekeeping was thus closely connected to observing the sky.

Read the documentary study ↗

Telescopes and measurements

The same study analyzes observations of Jupiter's satellites intended to refine geographical longitude, as well as magnetic declination measurements. Documents help reconstruct practices even when the identity of the observers remains debated.

Read the research ↗

Research into telescopes, clocks, compasses, sextants and other historical instruments should compare inventories, inscriptions, photographs and records of use. These examples guide research; they are not an inventory of objects currently on display.

Source: OAN Heritage Group ↗.

This website does not replace a museum catalogue. An object's identification, maker, date, provenance and condition must be supported by its documentary record.

A heritage that is also alive

The garden restoration, planned in 2024 and carried out in 2025, resulted from a partnership between the OAN and Bogotá's Botanical Garden. The project introduced over 1,400 plants, including six new trees, and renewed the site's symbolic dimension.

The design connects species associated with botanical tradition with the memory of Mutis, Humboldt and Garavito. It links astronomy, cultural landscape and environmental education.

Source: Vargas Domínguez and colleagues: garden restoration, 2026 ↗.

How to research this heritage

Locate

Start with the catalogue and record the title, date, author, collection and reference number. A bibliographic record does not mean that the document has been digitized.

Compare

Distinguish the document's date from the event's date. Compare manuscripts, publications and studies before attributing an observation or object.

Cite and preserve

Ask the custodian about consultation and reproduction conditions. Preserve the provenance of images and transcriptions so others can verify them.

The OAN Heritage Group works on tangible and intangible heritage. For enquiries about collections and projects, use the contact details in its profile.
